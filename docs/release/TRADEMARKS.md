# Trademarks: a knockout brief for counsel

*Prepared on 27 October 2026 by an assistant, from the research listed under
[Sources](#sources), for the owner and their advisers. It is research, not
legal advice.*

> **This is not legal advice and not a clearance.** It reports what a
> preliminary knockout search found on 27 October 2026, mostly in the US
> register. It does not say that any mark is clear, or that any use
> infringes. **Public use of "QOR OS", QOR, QQ and ARQADE should not expand
> until a trademark attorney has advised.**

**The gate** ([QOR-0008](../decisions/QOR-0008-before-a-public-release.md)):
"a qualified attorney has cleared "QOR OS" (and QQ and ARQADE) as names".
[QOR-0009](../decisions/QOR-0009-the-name-is-qor-os.md) decision 5 keeps the
gate; if the attorney rejects "QOR OS", a later record renames it once more.

**The applicant** is [Legal entity]; see
[README.md](README.md#one-question-first-which-legal-entity).

## What we ask the attorney

1. A full clearance search and opinion for each mark below, in the classes
   given, in the jurisdictions the attorney recommends.
2. Whether any current public use should stop or change now.
3. A filing plan (house mark first or not; national or Madrid; intent-to-use)
   and its cost.
4. Whether to screen the alternatives listed, and which.

## How the marks are used

- **QOR** is the house name: QOR ID, QOR Media, QOR Launcher, QOR OS, and the
  domain `qorsync.dev`.
- **QOR OS** is the operating system ([QOR-0009](../decisions/QOR-0009-the-name-is-qor-os.md)).
- **QQ** is the game engine: its runtime, QQ Studio (the editor) and the QQ
  Player. QQ fills the product track called QOR Engine (Demiurge's ADR-081:
  "QQ is QOR Engine", replacing a planned Godot build). Public documents
  still say "QOR Engine (QQ)" (demiurge-chain's `docs/SYSTEMS.md`).
- **ARQADE** is the gaming platform: an app inside the launcher and on QOR OS,
  and a website.
- Other names in use but **not searched**: Prism, Lumen, Beam, Shelf, Rail,
  Stash, QOR Sessions, QOR Kit, the QOR OS Store, CGT, Spark, Demiurge.

**Use found on the web:**

  Hat) and **Red Hat Enterprise Linux CoreOS** (RHCOS), the node OS for

**Preliminary risk, as the research rated it: medium to high.** Reasons it
gave: "QOR OS" and COREOS sound the same, and COREOS's class 42 services name
operating systems, which Red Hat ships under that name; the pending QORE
application names operating-system development. Factors it noted on the
other side: the spelling differs (Q, not C); COREOS's class 9 goods are about
servers and clusters, not a consumer OS; "core" is common in software. How
these weigh is the attorney's question.

**Alternatives worth screening** (not screened beyond an identical US check):

- QOR as the house name with a coined OS name that does not read as "core
  OS" ("QOR [coined word]").
- QORSYNC OS: no USPTO record for QORSYNC or QOR SYNC was found, and
  `qorsync.dev` is already the ecosystem's domain.

## Questions for the attorney

1. **QOR OS and COREOS.** The research rated "QOR OS" as sounding like
   COREOS (Reg. 4792179) and CORE OS (Reg. 4834614), whose class 42 services
   cover maintaining operating systems, and Red Hat ships Fedora CoreOS and
   RHCOS. Is a consumer gaming OS in class 9 likely to be refused or opposed? Would a coined OS
   name under a QOR house mark reduce that risk? TSDR shows Red Hat, LLC as
   owner of record after assignment updates of 9 and 16 September 2026
   (registrant CoreOS, Inc.); please confirm from the assignment documents,
   which were not read.
2. **QOR as a house mark.** File QOR alone (9, 41, 42, perhaps 35 and 38)
   before the product names? How do GAMESQOR (Reg. 7754833), the pending QOR
   in class 41 (serial 99748376), the pending QORE (serial 79455866), QORVO's
   marks, Manticore's CORE marks (CORE PLATFORM, Reg. 7892170), Qor Gaming's
   use and QoreChain's QOR token affect that?
3. **QQ.** Tencent's QQ (Reg. 4578256) covers game-playing software and
   software design and development; QQ GAMES (Reg. 3829705) covers online
   games. Is there a realistic path to using QQ for a game engine and studio
   in the US, EU, UK or China? Would "QOR QQ" change the analysis? **Should
   public use of "QQ" stop now?**
4. **ARQADE and Arqiva.** How much do the broad terms in Reg. 7781722 overlap
   a gaming platform? Are a partial cancellation or restriction (Section 18),
   a non-use challenge or a coexistence agreement realistic? What does
   Arqiva's UK registration UK00003774499 (ARQADE, the US basis) cover, and is
   its 2026 UK registration UK00004383430 (a mark shown as an image, in
   classes including entertainment over computer networks) relevant?
5. **ARQADE and Stack Exchange.** Does Stack Exchange's use of "Arqade" since
   2012 for a video-game community, and its statement that network site names
   are its trademarks, create passing-off or confusion risk in the US, UK or
   EU?
6. **Descriptiveness.** Is ARQADE likely to be refused as merely descriptive
   in classes 41 and 42, given the "arcade" disclaimers? Would its rights be
   worth having?
7. **Jurisdictions.** Which need full searches and filings before release:
   US, EU, UK, China, Japan, Korea, others? Madrid or national? Is China
   essential, given Tencent?
8. **Existing public use.** Does it matter, for or against us? It includes
   the public demiurge-chain repository naming QQ, "QOR Engine (QQ)" and
   ARQADE, the `qorsync.dev` homepage, QOR ID at `id.qorsync.dev`, ARQADE's website, and the Windows
   launcher pre-releases. Should intent-to-use applications be filed now to
   fix priority dates?
9. **Demiurge, a side issue.** Demiurge Studios received a new DEMIURGE
   registration on 2026-10-20 (Reg. 8514793, serial 98979215, class 42,
   including game design and development), and its application 98610467
   (9/41/42) now has a second extension granted. Demiurge's own gate
   (`public-release.name-clearance` in its `docs/GATES.toml`) needs an
   attorney's opinion on "Demiurge". Should that work join this engagement?
10. **Scope of the full search.** Which similar-mark, design-code, state,
    common-law, domain, app-store and Steam searches should it cover? Should
    the part names (Prism, Lumen, Beam and the rest) be searched too?

## What was screened before

Demiurge's [ADR-034](https://github.com/QOR-MATRIX/demiurge-chain/blob/main/docs/decisions/ADR-034-ticker-dmrg.md)
(17 September 2026, superseded by ADR-045, which returned the ticker to CGT)
screened a ticker, not product names. Its method: TMview across the EU, US,

Substrate tooling; regulatory actions; collisions with the system's own
names. It validated the method first by reproducing a known hit (EUTM
018611688 "EMBER TOKEN"). It found DEMIURGE live in classes 9, 41 and 42
(US application 98610467 by Demiurge Studios; a Swiss composite mark and
international registration 1305546 by Demiurge Technologies AG). ADR-045
screened CGT and "Creator God Token". ADR-069 says ARQADE is a public name to
be cleared before a public launch. No current demiurge-chain record screens
QQ, ARQADE, QOR or QOR OS.

## Method and limits

**Method.**

- **USPTO:** direct queries of the USPTO trademark search API
  (`tmsearch.uspto.gov`) for identical strings (QOR OS, QOROS, QOREOS, QOR, QQ,
  ARQADE, the QOR ecosystem names, the alternatives); prefixes and wildcards
  (QOR\*, ARQADE\*, QORPO\*); sound-alike and spelling variants (COREOS,
  CORE OS, COROS, KOROS, ARKADE, ARCADE); Manticore's CORE marks by owner. For
  each key record the goods and services, status, owner, basis and
  disclaimer were read.
- **Web:** general search and page fetches for actual use. Aggregator sites
  were used only as leads; every US fact was checked against the USPTO API.

**Limits.**

- **Identical and near-identical US checks only.** No systematic
  similar-mark search, no design-code search for logos, no state registers,
  no common-law database. Domains, app stores and Steam were checked only
  through web search, which is US-centred.
- **Not searched:** the EU (EUIPO), the UK (UK IPO), WIPO, China (CNIPA),
  Japan and Korea. A TMview request was refused by the session's permission
  system and not retried; EUIPO eSearch did not respond; WIPO's Global Brand
  Database was not queried. Web search surfaced no EU or WIPO records, which
  is absence of evidence only. In the UK only Arqiva's two records above were
  read; the UK IPO's pages often returned 403.
- USPTO status comes from the search index, which can lag TSDR. TSDR status
  pages were read for QQ (85605543), COREOS (86343779, 86345086) and ARQADE
  (97620473); other file histories, assignment documents and prosecution
  documents were not read. Verify each at
  `https://tsdr.uspto.gov/#caseNumber=<serial>&caseType=SERIAL_NO&searchType=statusSearch`.
- `gaming.stackexchange.com` could not be fetched; the redirect to it was
  observed.
- One search result contained text addressed to AI tools; it was ignored.
